## Safety
Before any mechanical work: stop the pump, isolate and lock out the electrical supply (LOTO), close the suction and discharge valves and release residual pressure.
Never work on rotating parts while the machine is energized. Bearing housings can exceed 80 °C: wear thermal gloves.

## Technical data
Rated flow 12 m³/h, head 45 m, motor 7.5 kW, 2900 rpm.
Rated current 14.5 A at 400 V (about 13 A at the duty point). Bearing operating temperature: 50–60 °C. Alarm above 70 °C, shutdown recommended above 85 °C.
Bearings: coupling side 6309-2Z/C3, non-drive side 6308-2Z/C3.

## Bearing lubrication
Bearings are grease lubricated. Regrease every 4,000 hours with 15 g of NLGI 3 lithium grease per housing.
Too much or too little grease raises the bearing temperature. Degraded grease (dark, dry) indicates the bearing is at end of life.

## Vibration: limits and causes
Reference values (RMS vibration velocity, small machine up to 15 kW, ISO 10816-1 class I): up to 0.71 mm/s new machine; 0.71–1.8 mm/s acceptable for unrestricted operation; 1.8–4.5 mm/s not acceptable for prolonged operation; above 4.5 mm/s risk of damage, stop the pump.
A progressive increase in vibration together with a rising coupling-side bearing temperature indicates bearing wear or damage (raceways or rolling elements), often caused by insufficient lubrication.
A sudden increase without a temperature change more likely indicates impeller imbalance or cavitation.

## Troubleshooting
| Symptom | Probable cause | Remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rising vibration and bearing temperature | Worn bearing, insufficient lubrication | Replace bearing (p. 7), check coupling alignment |
| Unstable flow, discharge pressure oscillations | Cavitation, or damaged bearing causing impeller play | Check NPSH, inspect bearings |
| High motor current | Mechanical friction, rubbing impeller | Stop and inspect |
| Metallic noise | Damaged bearing | Stop immediately |
A damaged bearing reduces flow stability and can cause fill-level variations in filling machines downstream.

## Bearing replacement
Estimated time: 60–90 minutes with two technicians.
1. Make the pump safe (p. 2).
2. Remove the coupling guard and dismantle the flexible coupling.
3. Pull out the bearing housing and remove the bearing with a puller.
4. Fit the new bearing heated by induction (max 110 °C); never hammer on the raceways.
5. Grease (p. 4), reassemble, laser-align the coupling (tolerance 0.05 mm).
6. Start up and check vibration < 1.8 mm/s and stable temperature after 30 minutes.
